HP weights aren't pulled from a hat. The values that combine six
on-chain signals into a single score were fit against historical
launches on Base, leakage-checked, and corroborated by published
research on adjacent launchpads. This page is the *why*.

For the active weights, see [HP scoring](/how-it-works/hp-scoring).

## How the weights were derived

The empirical fit ran in four iterations across spring 2026, each
correcting blind spots in the previous one.

The first run, on a 48-token sample, established that holder
concentration carried real predictive signal — settling whether to
ship a sixth component at all. The second run scaled to 250 tokens
and immediately exposed two broken inputs and three leakage flags
where a component's input was the same field deciding its outcome
label. The third run was the lock decision: a stratified 50/50
sample of survivor and dead tokens (n=100). Two findings dominated
that run:

- Momentum had no fittable signal. Regularized regression assigned
  it 0% weight on every outcome label tested. The diagnostic showed
  42% of survivor-half tokens had no informative delta in the
  measurement window — the window itself was structurally too narrow
  for the metric to fire.
- Sticky liquidity dominated the non-leakage components. RandomForest
  importance scored it 0.38 vs the next-highest at 0.26. The fit
  suggested sticky liquidity should sit much higher than the prior
  default of 20%.

The conservative read: take momentum's retired 10pp slot and give
it to sticky liquidity. Hold every other component at default.
That's the only weight change adopted.

The fourth run was an out-of-sample validation cohort — top tokens
by current FDV from a different sampling algorithm. It came back
small (n=7, a sampling-failure mode that's queued for a follow-up
fix) and slightly favored the pre-momentum-drop weights. Neither
result was significant at that sample size, but the direction is
enough to flag: the momentum drop is the most fragile of the
adopted changes.

## Cross-validation against published research

Two independent analyses of pump.fun, on a different chain with
different launchpad mechanics, surface the same dominant predictors
the HP design targets. That's the load-bearing piece — not that
specific coefficients transfer, but that the *kind* of signal we're
measuring is what other researchers find dominant.

Marino, Naviglio, Tarantelli, Lillo (2026), *Predicting the
success of new crypto-tokens: the Pump.fun case*
([arxiv 2602.14860](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14860), n=567,876).
Three findings that line up with the lock:

- *"Fast accumulation of liquidity through a small number of
  trades is the strongest predictor of graduation."* Matches
  sticky liquidity as the dominant non-leakage component.
- *"Markets dominated by bot-like activity exhibit systematically
  lower graduation probabilities."* Matches the holder-concentration
  HHI penalty and the sqrt-dampening on effective buyers.
- *"Presence of historically successful traders provides at most
  a modest and non-monotonic effect."* Confirms the design
  decision not to model cross-token wallet history.

The wangr.com [pump.fun dashboard](https://wangr.com/analysis/pumpfun)
(n=567,876) reports ~3–5% 7-day survival on pump.fun. The observed
4.5% base survivor rate on Base lands inside that band — different
chain, different mechanics, similar mortality dynamics.

## The locked weights

| Component | Weight | What it measures |
|---|---:|---|
| velocity | 30% | Decayed net buy inflow with sybil dampening |
| effectiveBuyers | 15% | sqrt-dampened headcount of meaningful buyers |
| stickyLiquidity | 30% | Time-weighted LP depth penalised by recent withdrawals |
| retention | 15% | Long+short two-anchor holder retention fraction |
| holderConcentration | 10% | HHI-mapped holder distribution |
| momentum | 0% | Recent base-composite delta (currently disabled) |

Sum: 100%. Sticky liquidity moved up 10pp from default to absorb
momentum's retired slot. Every other coefficient is at its prior
default.

## Caveats

- The validation cohort came back at n=7 and slightly favored
  momentum-included weights (ρ +0.679 vs +0.571). Neither result is
  significant, but it's enough to call the momentum drop the most
  fragile of the changes.
- The lock holds for mainnet on an asymmetric-risk argument: if it's
  wrong, off-chain weights make a runtime detection plus refit cheap;
  if the prior defaults were correct and we shipped them, we'd be
  running mis-tuned weights without an obvious detection path.
- Two leakage suspects survived to the final fit (sticky liquidity vs
  price-floor outcome at ρ=+0.87; retention vs holder-retention at
  ρ=+0.94). Neither component is boosted beyond the conservative
  +10pp; the leakage flag is the reason.

## How weight changes work

Weights are off-chain config, not contract bytecode. Contracts read
an oracle-signed Merkle root of rankings, not the coefficients — so
any change is enforceable only when the oracle reposts a new root
under the new set.

A weight change requires an empirical fit on at least 200 tokens,
external operator review, seven days of public notice, and an
indexer redeploy with a new version string. Every snapshot row the
indexer writes is tagged with the version that scored it, so
historical scores stay re-derivable.

The trade-off is intentional: refitting against real data without
redeploying contracts, in exchange for a public notice and
version-stamping discipline anyone can audit.

## What's next

A follow-up empirical run is in flight using a different cohort
algorithm — walk recent on-chain swap activity globally, rank by
volume, sample only the top-N. That should clear the n=7
sampling-failure mode and either confirm or revisit the momentum
drop. Once the protocol has 90+ days of its own mainnet data, the
right corpus to refit against is its own. A possible revival of
momentum at 5–10% is on the table if a wider delta-window
experiment surfaces signal — the compute path is preserved behind
a feature flag for exactly that contingency.

This page will be updated when those land.
