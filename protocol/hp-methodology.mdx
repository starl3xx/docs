## Why this page exists

filter.fun's HP score determines who gets filtered. The weight values
that combine the six on-chain signals into a single number aren't
pulled from a hat — they're empirically fit against historical token
launches on Base, leakage-checked, and cross-validated against
published academic research on adjacent launchpads. This page documents
how those weights were derived, what was tried and discarded, and the
caveats we're shipping with.

If you came here to verify *what* is currently active, that lives at
[`/how-it-works/hp-scoring`](/how-it-works/hp-scoring). This page is
the *why*.

> The summary: drop momentum from the HP composite, redistribute its
> 10pp weight to stickyLiquidity, hold every other component at spec
> default. Locked 2026-05-03 as `HP_WEIGHTS_VERSION = "2026-05-03-v4-locked"`.

## The story arc — Track E v2 → v3 → v4 → validation cohort

The HP weight study ran in four iterations across spring 2026, each
correcting blind spots in the previous run. The recommendation that
landed in the spec is the product of all four — not a single fit.

| Run | n | Headline finding | What changed |
|---|---:|---|---|
| v2 (PR #59) | 48 | First real signal; holderConcentration validated as predictive | Confirmed the 6th component is safe to ship |
| v3 (PR #62) | 250 | Two component inputs broken; outcome leakage; momentum 98% zero | Fetcher fixes + leakage flags + momentum diagnostic dispatched |
| v4 (PR #66) | 100 stratified | Momentum has no fittable signal; stickyLiquidity dominates non-leakage components; corroborates Marino | §6.5 weights LOCKED |
| v4 validation (PR #76) | 7 | Pre-v4 defaults slightly out-correlate LOCKED at this n; lock held with caveat | v5 follow-up filed |
| v5 (PR TBD) | target 50–200 | TBD — liquidity-first cohort algorithm in flight | Lock will be validated, revisited, or held inconclusive |

### v2 — the first real signal

Track E v2 ran the analysis pipeline on a 48-token corpus pulled from
Clanker V4 launches. The headline result wasn't a weight value — it was
that `holderConcentration`, the sixth component proposed in spec §41,
showed a real correlation with downstream token health. That settled
the "should we even ship a 6th component" question. v2 didn't have
enough tokens to fit reliable coefficients, but it cleared the path
for v3.

### v3 — the breakage that exposed the pipeline

The v3 dispatch scaled the corpus to 250 tokens and immediately surfaced
a series of fetcher bugs: two of the seven HP-component inputs were
zero on >97% of tokens, the `momentum` input was structurally degraded
(zero on 98%), and three outcome labels were leaking — i.e. the input
that scored a component was the same field that decided the outcome
label, producing a misleading correlation. v3 didn't lock anything;
it produced a punch list. The leakage flags became the standard
discipline for v4.

### v4 — the lock

v4 ran a stratified 50/50 survivor/dead corpus of n=100. The
stratification matters: if you only sample tokens that survived, the
fit can't learn what predicts survival. By forcing a balanced sample,
v4 gave the regression and the RandomForest something real to fit.
Two findings drove the lock:

1. **Momentum had no fittable signal.** L2-regularized logistic
   regression assigned momentum 0% weight on every outcome label
   tested. The `hp_delta_recent` diagnostic showed 42% of survivor-half
   tokens had no informative delta in the 72h→96h window — the
   measurement window itself is structurally too narrow for the
   metric to fire.
2. **stickyLiquidity is the dominant non-leakage component.** Even
   discounting the leakage-flagged ρ=+0.87 against `price_floor`,
   stickyLiquidity scored RF importance 0.38 vs the next-highest
   component (velocity) at 0.26. The empirical fit suggested stickyLiquidity
   should sit much higher than the spec's 20%.

The conservative read: take momentum's retired 10pp slot and give it
to stickyLiquidity. That's the only weight change v4 makes.

### v4 validation cohort — the asterisk

The validation cohort run was meant to corroborate the lock against an
independent slice — top tokens by current FDV, drawn from a different
sampling algorithm than the main corpus. It came back at n=7 (a
sampling-failure mode documented in the report; the candidate-then-FDV
funnel is too sparse for a 765k-token population) and the resulting
rank correlation slightly *favored pre-v4 spec defaults* over LOCKED:

- LOCKED (Scenario B, momentum=0): ρ = +0.571
- Pre-v4 defaults (momentum=10%): ρ = +0.679

Neither result is significant at n=7, but the direction is enough to
flag: momentum's drop is the most fragile of the four LOCKED weight
changes. The report's recommendation, accepted: hold the lock for
mainnet, file a v5 dispatch with a better cohort algorithm, and be
ready to revisit if v5 corroborates the validation cohort.

### v5 — what's coming

The v5 dispatch swaps the candidate-then-FDV-sample funnel for a
liquidity-first scan: walk PoolManager swap logs over the last 7
days, rank pools by recent activity, FDV-sample only the top-N
cross-referenced subset. Estimated runtime ≈10–15 minutes for ~50–200
tokens with actual recent on-chain liveness — the regime where
momentum signal, if it exists, should be detectable. This page will
be updated when v5 lands.

## Methodology

The end-to-end pipeline is implemented in
[`track-e/pipeline.py`](https://github.com/starl3xx/filter-fun/blob/main/track-e/pipeline.py)
and is reproducible end-to-end against a deterministic synthetic
corpus before being run on real data.

1. **Stratified corpus.** Pull a balanced sample of survivor +
   dead tokens from Clanker V4 launches in a 90d → 8d ago discovery
   window. v4 used a 50/50 mix from 1,106 candidates. Stratification
   prevents survivor bias: if you only look at tokens that survived,
   you can't learn what predicts surviving.

2. **Compute HP components** for each token at t+96h post-launch
   using the spec §6.4 implementations — same code paths the
   production indexer uses. The three unbounded components
   (velocity, effectiveBuyers, stickyLiquidity) are normalized via
   corpus-relative percentile rank so the composite is comparable
   across tokens.

3. **Compute outcome labels.** Forward-replay each token to 30d /
   60d / 90d horizons and tag binary outcomes: did this token retain
   holders? Did the price hold above a floor? Did weekly volume
   stay above a threshold? Critically, outcome labels are computed
   from data the HP components don't read at scoring time — that's
   what eliminates the v3-flagged leakage class.

4. **Fit weights.** L2-regularized logistic regression assigns a
   non-negative coefficient to each component per outcome label,
   normalized to sum to 1.0. Compare against spec defaults.

5. **RandomForest importance** as a non-linear sanity check on the
   linear fit. If a feature looks important to the linear fit but
   trivially rankable by a tree, the fit is probably overfit to a
   monotone relationship the data doesn't actually support.

6. **Leakage discipline.** Flag any component-vs-outcome correlation
   with |ρ| ≥ 0.85. Treat the corresponding fit weights as inflated.
   This is the single most important addition v4 made over v3 — the
   v3 fit suggested stickyLiquidity should be 83% of the composite
   on the leakage-suspect outcome label; the leakage-aware reading
   gives a much more conservative picture.

7. **Lock decision.** Only adopt fitted weights that survive the
   leakage check, have non-trivial empirical signal *and* don't
   disrupt rank stability vs current weights. v4's locked set
   produces ρ ≥ +0.99 rank correlation with both the spec defaults
   and the fitted weights — i.e., the change is small in actual
   ranking impact, which is the point.

## Cross-validation against published research

This is the most credibility-bearing section, so we'll be precise
about what the external data shows and what it doesn't.

filter.fun runs on Base, uses Uniswap V4 hooks, has a 200-bps fee
split, and locks creator allocations. None of those match pump.fun's
mechanics. So the cross-validation we want from external sources isn't
"these specific weight values transfer," it's "*the dominant
predictors filter.fun is targeting are the same ones independent
research finds dominant on adjacent launchpads.*"

### Marino, Naviglio, Tarantelli, Lillo (2026)

> *"Predicting the success of new crypto-tokens: the Pump.fun case"*
> — [arxiv 2602.14860](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14860).

The Marino paper analyzes n=567,876 pump.fun tokens. Three of its
findings line up directly with Track E v4's:

- *"Fast accumulation of liquidity through a small number of trades
  is the strongest predictor of graduation"* — matches our
  `stickyLiquidity` as the dominant non-leakage component.
- *"Markets dominated by bot-like activity exhibit systematically
  lower graduation probabilities"* — matches our `holderConcentration`
  HHI penalty for concentrated holdings, and our `effectiveBuyers`
  sqrt-dampening that discounts bot-style burst-buying.
- *"Presence of historically successful traders provides at most a
  modest and non-monotonic effect"* — confirms our scope decision
  *not* to model cross-token wallet history. This was a real
  consideration in the v2 design phase; Marino is independent
  evidence we drew the boundary in the right place.

We were unable to confirm one Marino finding within v4's corpus:
the partition for `velocity ≥ p75 ∩ effectiveBuyers ≤ p25` (Marino's
"sniper" pattern) returned zero tokens in our survivor half — the
corner is empty. Two readings: either Clanker V4's locker + dynamic-fee
hooks structurally block the sniper-dump pattern that pump.fun's
bonding curve allowed (a *substantive* mechanism difference), or the
corpus is too small (n=50 in the survivor half) for a 25%×25% subset
to land non-empty. v5 will retest at larger n.

### wangr.com pump.fun dashboard

> [wangr.com/analysis/pumpfun](https://wangr.com/analysis/pumpfun)

The wangr dashboard reports n=567,876 pump.fun tokens with mortality
+ holder + graduation data. Headline numbers: ~12-day average lifespan,
~0.5% graduation rate, ~3–5% 7-day survival rate. Track E v4's
observed survivor rate of 4.5% on Clanker V4 lands inside that band —
the launchpad-mortality side of the analysis is consistent across
venues. Different chain, different launchpad mechanics, similar
mortality dynamics.

### Why these matter

filter.fun is on Base, not Solana, and uses Uniswap V4 hooks instead
of pump.fun's bonding curve. Two independent analyses on different
chains and different launchpad mechanics arriving at the same
dominant predictors — fast liquidity accumulation, bot-activity
penalty, no signal from cross-token wallet history — is strong
evidence that the HP design is targeting the right *kind* of signal,
not just an artifact of the specific Clanker V4 corpus we sampled.

Cross-validation does *not* establish that the specific coefficient
values transfer. That's what v5 and the post-mainnet refit are for.

## The locked weights

`HP_WEIGHTS_VERSION = "2026-05-03-v4-locked"`. Spec ref §6.5.

| Component | Weight | Δ vs pre-v4 | Spec § |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| velocity | 30% | — | §6.4.1 |
| effectiveBuyers | 15% | — | §6.4.2 |
| stickyLiquidity | **30%** | **+10pp** | §6.4.3 |
| retention | 15% | — | §6.4.4 |
| momentum | **0%** | **−10pp (REMOVED)** | §6.4.5 |
| holderConcentration | 10% | — | §6.4.6, §41 |
| **Total** | **100%** | | |

Per-component rationale (high-level — link out to spec §6.4 for the
formulas):

- **velocity** — decayed net buy inflow with sybil dampening and
  churn discount. Held at 30%: the empirical fit suggested 19% but
  the leakage-aware reading of v4 doesn't support a deviation from
  spec at the corpus size we have.
- **effectiveBuyers** — sqrt-dampened headcount of meaningful buyers.
  Held at 15%: same reasoning as velocity. The sqrt-dampening is the
  load-bearing piece of the design here, not the coefficient.
- **stickyLiquidity** — time-weighted LP depth with recent-withdrawal
  penalty. Raised to 30% from 20% (+10pp): only weight that moved.
  Absorbs the momentum slot. Both v4 fits and Marino's "fast
  liquidity accumulation" finding support a higher coefficient than
  the spec default; the leakage flag means we don't go all the way
  to the L2 fit's 42%.
- **retention** — long+short two-anchor holder retention fraction.
  Held at 15%: the L2 fit assigned 59% but flagged retention as
  leakage-suspect against the holder_retention outcome.
- **momentum** — REMOVED. Coefficient zeroed; the
  `HP_MOMENTUM_ENABLED` flag defaults to `false` so the compute
  path is entirely skipped. v5 may revive the component if a wider
  delta-window experiment surfaces signal.
- **holderConcentration** — HHI-mapped distribution penalty.
  Held at 10%: ρ=+0.43 is real signal but n=100 with 61% zero-holder
  tokens is too small to support raising it.

## Honest caveats

The version of v4 that's shipping is the most defensible reading
of the data we have, *not* the most aggressive one. A few things
we want to be plain about:

- **The validation cohort came back at n=7.** Pre-v4 spec defaults
  edged out LOCKED (ρ +0.679 vs +0.571) on this cohort. Neither
  result reaches significance at that n, but the direction is
  enough to flag that the momentum drop may be sensitive to the
  population slice — recent-active tokens may be exactly the regime
  where momentum signal exists, and that regime was
  under-represented in the main corpus.
- **The lock held for mainnet on an asymmetric-risk argument.**
  If LOCKED is wrong, off-chain weights mean a runtime detection
  + refit is cheap (re-fit, bump the version constant, redeploy
  the indexer). If we shipped pre-v4 defaults and the main-corpus
  signal was correct, we'd be running mis-tuned weights without
  an obvious detection path. Asymmetry favors LOCKED.
- **Momentum's drop is the most fragile of the four weight
  changes.** It may be revived at 5–10% in v5/v6 if the wider
  delta-window experiment surfaces signal.
- **Two leakage suspects survived to the v4 fit.** stickyLiquidity
  vs price_floor (ρ=+0.87) and retention vs holder_retention
  (ρ=+0.94). The lock proposal explicitly does not boost either
  component to the L2-fitted value; the leakage flag is the reason.
- **Marino's sniper pattern was indeterminate, not refuted.** The
  empty cell is consistent with V4 mechanics blocking the pattern
  *or* with the corpus being too small. Don't read absence as evidence.

## How weight revisions work

Weights are off-chain config, not contract bytecode. This is the
critical design choice that makes the methodology work — and the
critical accountability promise that has to come with it.

- The active set lives at
  [`packages/scoring/src/types.ts`](https://github.com/starl3xx/filter-fun/blob/main/packages/scoring/src/types.ts)
  as `LOCKED_WEIGHTS`. Contracts never read it.
- Every `hpSnapshot` row the indexer writes is tagged with
  `HP_WEIGHTS_VERSION` for historical reproducibility.
- Live weights are queryable at
  [`/scoring/weights`](/api-reference/season) (returns the version
  string, the coefficient set, the activation timestamp, and the
  feature-flag bundle).
- Weight changes require: spec §6.5 amendment + empirical
  justification on a corpus of ≥200 tokens + ≥7-day public notice
  + indexer redeploy. The full procedure is documented at
  [`/how-it-works/hp-scoring`](/how-it-works/hp-scoring).
- Anyone can verify what's running at any time. The accountability
  surface is the oracle's per-version Merkle-root commitments — if
  the published `weightsVersion` on a settlement-authoritative row
  doesn't match the value at `/scoring/weights`, that's a
  detectable drift.

The trade-off is intentional: we get the ability to refit against
mainnet data without redeploying contracts, in exchange for a public
notice + version-stamping discipline that anyone can audit.

## Future work

- **Track E v5** — liquidity-first cohort algorithm. In flight at
  time of writing. Should clear the n=7 sampling-failure mode and
  give us either confirmation or refutation of the LOCKED choices,
  particularly the momentum drop.
- **Post-mainnet refit** — once filter.fun has 90+ days of mainnet
  data, the right corpus to fit against is *our own data*, not
  Clanker V4's. The post-mainnet refit will explicitly retest
  every locked coefficient against the actual filter.fun cohort.
- **Possible momentum revival.** If the wider delta-window
  experiment (96h − 48h instead of 96h − 72h) surfaces a fittable
  signal, momentum may come back at 5–10%. The compute path is
  preserved behind a feature flag for exactly this contingency.
- **Per-phase weight differentiation.** Spec §6.5 reserves the
  Pre-filter / Finals / Default phases for distinct weight sets;
  v4's corpus was too small to fit three sets so it shipped with
  one. v5 (or the post-mainnet refit) may revisit if the data
  permits.
